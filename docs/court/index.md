## Overview
Court is an activity in Tucker Island that acts loosely similar to U.S. Court. It includes both Criminal and Civil trials that take place in the server, with verdicts that have interesting or funny charges.

Generally Court involves five major roles. These five include the following:

1. A judge
2. The jury
3. Prosecutor<span class="transparent">(s)</span>
4. Lawyer<span class="transparent">(s)</span>
5. A defendant <sub><span class="transparent">(Plaintiff's are also called defendants).</span></sub>

Without these components Court cannot function as intended. It's a party activity that requires the cooperation of numerous members in the server.

## Proceedings
First a trial must be established. This means a criminal or civil trial must first be created for Court to commence.

`How are trials created?`

:   As we know there are two types of trials: criminal and civil. A criminal trial is created after a member of the server commits a crime according to the list of [misdeamenors and felonies][offense-list].<br/>
    A civil trial is created when a member of the server sues another member in the bounds of our [suing guidelines][sue-guide].

`When does a trial start?`

:   Trials are scheduled according to when others are free, and when that day & time is reached the trial will be carried out.

`How does Court play out?`

:   ### Intermission
    Court officially begins when all essential members of the trial join the session. From there, each side will have ten minutes of intermission to organize a game-plan, gather evidence, and review this website if need be. If the trial is a civil one, the prosecutor must state their reason for the trial and what they seek to gain out of it.

:   ### Round Start
    Both the defendant, and prosecutor will choose a side of a coin. Then the judge will flip a coin to decide who goes first. If the judge is incapable of flipping a coin, they will pick a member of the jury to do so.

    The chosen party will begin their statements, claims, and overall argument. They are allowed to bring up witnesses, present evidence, cross-examine, object to the opposing side, challenge their credibility, impeach evidence, request time extensions, and do really anything you'd expect in court. The opposing party is generally not allowed to speak, but lawyers do have the abiltiy to do callouts like, <span class="transparent">(e.g.)</span> "heresay".

:   ### Round Cycles
    Each round will cap at ten minutes. Throughout the round the jury is allowed to request to speak, and speak into the chat to make comments on the argument and other things. However the opposing side in the trial does not have permission to speak. There are a minimum of four rounds total in a trial. After each round the opposing side gets priority to make their argument for the next. If after the set four rounds a compelling amount of time extension requests, or requests to extend how many rounds there are exist; the judge can add an additional two, four, six, eight (etc.) rounds to the trial.

:   ### Vulnerabilities
    1. Every round only two witnesses can be brought up.
    2. Evidence submissions are limited to four per round.
    3. Find <i>loopholes</i> in this documentation if you want an advantage on your case.

:   ### Conclusion
    After all the rounds have played out. The defendant and the prosecutor, plus their accomplices will be unable to talk for the rest of the session. The judge and jury will come to a final consensus on what charges the defendant should face, if any.
    
    Then court will be released and the charges may be applied. The judge can also rule additional proceedings at the end of the trial if they wish to do so. For example, like an extra trial of justifiable paramaters.
